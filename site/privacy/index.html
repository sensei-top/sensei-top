Privacy Policy

Last changed 2026-11-05. That date is also this policy's version.

Sensei Top works fully without an account. A free, optional account adds cloud backup of your match history and error reports. Your matches, decks and reports live in one database on your own computer, and leave it only when you say yes.

Each of these is a yes of its own, given after you sign in. Report a problem sends one report each time you press it and agree, with or without an account. You can also contribute your games, with or without an account: the app removes everything that names you or your opponent, then sends them to a private pool that Sensei Top's own features are built from. Contributing is a yes of its own too, and needs no account. An AI rules coach is on its way; the sections marked Not available yet describe how it will work, and nothing in them happens today.

This page lists every request the app makes on its own, what each one carries, what this website does, what the account collects, and what contributing your games sends.

Who we are

Sensei Top is made by Gabriel Quirino Ferreira Rocha, an individual in Brazil. "We" and "us" on this page mean him. He decides how the data on this page is used (the "controller" under Brazil's LGPD and the EU's GDPR) and is the data-protection contact (the "encarregado" under the LGPD).

Write to contact@senseitop.com for anything on this page. For a vulnerability, read SECURITY.md first.

The short version

  • The app works fully without an account.
  • Your match history stays on your computer unless you choose cloud backup or contributing your games.
  • Each use of your data behind the account is a separate choice. None is pre-ticked, and you can undo each one at any time.
  • Contributing your games is a choice of its own, and needs no account. Saying yes sends the games already in your history as well as new ones. Nothing in a contributed game names you, so once a game is sent we cannot delete it, even with your account. You can stop at any time.
  • We never sell your data, never show you ads, and never use your data for advertising.
  • You can download all of your cloud data, and delete your account and everything in it, from the app.

What the app downloads

Each of these is a reference download: data in, nothing about you out. Every one fails softly, so the app keeps working without it.

  • Scryfall: the card database, once, and card images one at a time as the dashboard shows them. A request for a picture names that card, so Scryfall can see which cards a copy has shown, never when, against whom or with what result.
  • MTGOFormatData: the archetype definitions that name an opponent's deck.
  • MTG_decklistcache and mtgo.com: public tournament results for the Field page, in the formats you follow.
  • TopDeck.gg: its events, only if you add a free key of your own in Settings. The request carries that key and nothing else of yours.
  • GitHub: the version check, once a day and each time the app starts. It carries what any web request does, your IP address and the app's version among it, and nothing about your games. Check for updates in Settings turns it off.

Nothing about your games is sent to any of these, with the one exception Scryfall's line names.

The one call you ask for

A written guide or report can come from a prompt you copy into any assistant yourself, in which case the app sends nothing. If instead you set a Claude key of your own in Settings and press a generate button, the app sends that report's prompt to Anthropic's API under your key, shows the cost it measured, and stores the answer in your database. The cross-deck coach report can also ask Anthropic to search a short list of Magic websites; that search runs on Anthropic's servers, under your key. No key, no call; no press, no call. Nothing ever calls on a schedule.

That call is between you and Anthropic, under your own agreement with them. It never passes through any server of ours, and the account described below does not change it.

Where your data lives

Your match history is one database file in a folder of its own; Settings shows the folder and opens it. A key you add is kept in Windows Credential Manager or your system keyring, or in a settings file in your home folder when there is no keyring to use; it is never in the database. Uninstalling the app leaves the folder where it is, so reinstalling picks up where you left off; delete it yourself to remove everything.

The app can also make a diagnostics file for you to send us when something goes wrong. It is saved on your computer and goes nowhere unless you send it yourself. It leaves out your keys, your Magic Online name and your database. If you send us one, we use it only to fix your problem.

This website

This site is static files served by a Cloudflare Worker, and one small script, behind the Download buttons and in front of both home pages and every English page that has a Portuguese version. It sets no cookie. Beyond the page itself it makes two requests. One is Cloudflare Web Analytics, a cookieless count of page views that keeps no identifier of yours; it tells us how many people read this page, and nothing about who. The other happens only if your browser cannot reach the site: it may tell Cloudflare so, through Network Error Logging; that report says which page failed and from which network and country, and Cloudflare keeps no address of yours from it.

The Download buttons point at this site so that they always find the newest installer. When you press one, the script asks GitHub for the name of the newest release's installer; that request comes from Cloudflare and carries nothing of yours. It then sends your browser to that file on GitHub Releases, and the download itself comes from GitHub, which receives what any web request carries, your IP address and browser among it. The script keeps no log, sets no cookie and stores nothing. If GitHub cannot be reached, it sends you to the releases page instead.

The home page, in either language, also passes through the script, to put the installer for your computer first. Your browser names its system in every request it sends, as Windows, Linux, macOS or a phone's; the script reads that name, uses it for that one choice and nothing else, keeps no log of it and stores nothing.

Every English page that has a Portuguese version also passes through the script, to choose a language. Cloudflare already works out a country from the IP address of every request it serves; the script reads that country, not the address. If it is Brazil, and you did not come from another page of this site, the script sends you to the same page in Portuguese. Clicking English on a Portuguese page does not send you back, and the Portuguese pages are never redirected. To know whether you came from this site, your browser now tells it which page you came from when you move between its pages, and still tells no other site anything. The country is used for that one decision and nothing else; the script keeps no log of it and stores nothing.

If you write to us

If you email us, we keep your message and address to answer you and to follow up on the problem, and delete them 2 years after the conversation ends. We do not add you to any mailing list. Mail to contact@senseitop.com passes through Cloudflare Email Routing, which forwards it to a Gmail mailbox, so Cloudflare and Google handle it on the way and Google stores it for us.

Contributing your games

You can choose to send us your games, so that Sensei Top can learn from many players' games rather than only yours: which decks are doing well, how matchups go, how cards perform. Before a game leaves your computer, the app removes everything that names you or your opponent. Sent games go into a private pool that only Sensei Top's own features use.

A yes of its own. Contributing is never the price of the account. It works with or without one, the account works the same whether you say yes or no, and saying no costs you nothing. The app asks you once, the first time you open a version that has this choice. If you create an account in the app and have not said yes by then, the sign-up asks you again, as a question of its own. Nothing is pre-ticked. The app keeps your answer, that you confirmed your age, this policy's version and when you answered, on your computer.

Stopping. You can turn Help build the stats on or off at any time, in Settings. Turning it off stops it at once: no game is sent from then on, including any game still waiting to be sent. Turning it back on in Settings shows the question again unless you have already answered it, with your age confirmed, under this version of the policy.

Which games. Your yes covers the games already in your history as well as the games you play after it, and the question says so. If you turn it off and later back on, the games you played while it was off are sent too. A past game is sent the same way as a new one: the week it was played, never the day; your rank's class, never the step.

What is sent. For each game, the app builds a record of it on your computer, turn by turn, and sends that record: a new game once it ends, a past game from your history. The record holds:

  • whether the game was played on Arena or Magic Online, its format, whether it was best of one or best of three, and the kind of event: ranked, casual play, limited or another event, never the event's own name; a game against the computer or a Direct Challenge carries no event;
  • the week it was played, never the day or the time;
  • on Arena, your rank's class, such as Gold or Mythic, never the step within it or your Mythic place;
  • which game of the match it was, and whether you were on the play or the draw;
  • how many times you took a mulligan and, on Arena, the opening hand you kept;
  • your deck: the main deck and the sideboard, card names and counts;
  • for each turn: on Arena, the cards you drew and both players' life totals; on both, the lands each player played and the spells each player cast;
  • the result, and how many turns the game lasted;
  • the archetype names the app gave both decks: yours by the app's own rules; your opponent's as the app named it, or as you picked it when your pick is a known archetype or Rogue. A name you typed yourself is never sent, and neither is a description of a deck by its colours;
  • the version of this policy you said yes under, and the version of the record's own format;
  • a random code made fresh for that one game, so that we can tell records apart. It is not made from anything about you, the match or the game.

If a game's log does not record a detail, the game is sent without it; the app never guesses one.

The app remembers, on your computer, which games it has already sent, so it never sends one twice. It does not keep the code a game was sent under. If your opponent also contributes, their side of the same game is a separate record.

Never sent: your name or screen name, your opponent's name or screen name, chat, your Arena or Magic Online account or user ID, your Sensei Top account or installation ID, your deck's name, the event's own name, the match's ID in any form, the exact date or time of the game, file paths from your computer, and your Arena or Magic Online logs. Your opponent appears in the record only as the lands they played and the spells they cast, their life total on Arena, and the archetype name the app gave their deck, or the one you picked. Even when you are signed in, the app sends a game without your sign-in, so the upload does not say which account sent it. We do not record when a game arrives.

Your IP address. Like any request, an upload reaches our database at Supabase, in São Paulo, Brazil, from your IP address. We do not store it with the game. Supabase's request log keeps it for 1 day, as it does for every request.

Once sent, a game cannot be taken back. Nothing in a contributed game says who sent it, so we cannot tell which games are yours. We cannot show them to you, give you a copy, or delete them, and deleting your account does not remove them. The question in the app says this before you answer.

How we use them. Only to build Sensei Top's own features, such as metagame and matchup statistics and how cards perform. Players see what those features build from many games, never a single contributed game. We never sell the pool, never give it away, and never use it for advertising. We do not publish it. Publishing it, or using it in any way this section does not describe, would be a change to this policy, announced first: the app would ask you again before it sends another game, and the change would apply only to games sent after you agree.

Where it is stored: in our database at Supabase, in São Paulo, Brazil, kept apart from accounts, with no link to any of them. No player can read it.

Age. Contribute only if you are 13 or older and, if you are under 18, with a parent's or guardian's permission. The question asks you to confirm this.

Cloud backup of your match history

Applies only if you turn on Back up my match history.

Your computer's database stays the original. The cloud copy is a backup, and a way to move your history to another computer.

What is uploaded for each match: the match's ID from Arena or Magic Online, which of the two it was played on, when it was played (to the second), the event and format, whether it was best of one or best of three, your deck's name and list (main deck and sideboard, card names and counts), the result, games won and lost, whether you were on the play or the draw in the first game, the archetype of the opponent's deck when known, as the app named it or you picked it, the opponent's cards you saw, on Arena how long the match took, how many cards the app could not name, and any correction you made yourself (your pick of the opponent's deck, or of your own deck and format for a Magic Online match) with when you made it. Each upload also says which version of this record format it uses. A Magic Online match is backed up only once you have picked its deck.

Never uploaded: Arena's Player.log or Magic Online's game logs, your Arena or Magic Online account ID or screen name, your opponents' names, and chat.

About each computer: to keep your computers in step, we also store a random ID for each installation, the app version, the operating system, and when that installation last synced.

Only you can read your own match history. Our database enforces this for every row.

Error reports and Report a problem

Automatic error reports are sent only if you turn on Send error reports. A report says what went wrong: the error message and where in the code it happened, the app version, and your operating system and its version. A report from the app itself also carries the name and version of Sentry's reporting library and a few technical labels: that it came from a released build, how serious the error was, the platform, and which part of the app it came from. A report from the app's local server, which reads your database and serves the dashboard, also carries the Python version, the method of the request that failed and the route it was for, such as /api/matches/{match_id}: never the IDs in the request's address, its query or its contents. A request to an address the server does not know carries no route. When you are signed in, a report is linked to your account by your account's internal ID, never by your email address. Before a report leaves your computer, the app removes your user name from file paths, and removes email addresses, keys and tokens, Arena account IDs, Magic Online names and opponents' names. It never attaches your Player.log. Errors in the background process that reads your logs are not reported.

Report a problem is in the menu of the app's icon in the system tray, in Settings under App, on every error message, and on the screen shown when a page stops working; once the rules coach launches, it will also be under every coach answer. It works even when automatic reports are off, and even when you are not signed in: each press sends one report, and nothing else. When you are signed in, the report is linked to your account's internal ID; when you are not, it carries no account ID. You write what happened; opened from an error, the text starts with a line quoting the error's message, which you can change or delete. Like an automatic report, it carries the app version and your operating system. You can also choose to attach the last 500 lines of the app's own log (scrubbed the same way) and, for a coach answer, that question and answer. The log can name cards, decks and files and say when things happened. With Diagnostic mode on, it also holds the full text of any prompt the app sent to Claude.

Reports go to Sentry, in the United States, which stores them for us. Sentry is set not to store IP addresses. It still works out an approximate location from the address of each report before discarding the address. Once they launch, the coach website and the coach's server will report their errors the same way.

The rules coach

Not available yet. Applies once the rules coach launches, and needs an account.

The rules coach answers questions about Magic's rules and how cards interact, citing the rules it used. Its answers are written by an AI model.

What is sent, and where: your question goes to our coach server. The server finds the rules and card texts that match it, then sends your question and those texts to Anthropic, a US company, whose Claude models write the answer. With them goes a one-way code made from your account's internal ID, so Anthropic can help detect misuse coming from one account; Anthropic cannot turn it back into your ID. We do not send Anthropic your name, email address, or Google or Discord account ID. Please do not put personal information in a question.

How Anthropic handles it: Anthropic, PBC processes the question only to answer it, as our processor, under its Commercial Terms and its Data Processing Addendum. Its terms do not allow it to train its models on what we send, and we have not agreed to let it. Anthropic stores the question and its answer in the United States, and may process them in any country where it runs its models; it does not name those countries in advance. It deletes the question and its answer within 30 days. It may keep them longer only if its automated safety systems flag the question as breaking its Usage Policy (then for up to 2 years), or if the law requires. The coach sends each question on its own and does not ask Anthropic to store the conversation.

What we keep:

  • With Keep my coach history on: your questions and the answers, so you can reread them. You can delete them one by one, or all at once.
  • With it off: neither your question nor the answer. We keep only a row with when you asked, which model answered and what it cost.
  • Always: that row, for the daily limit and the monthly budget.

Feedback: each answer has a thumbs up, a thumbs down and Report this answer. A thumbs up or down is stored with the answer. A report sends that question and answer to us through Sentry, so we can read it and fix the coach. We may turn a reported question into a test for the coach, which is public; if we do, we rewrite it so it carries nothing of yours, and never publish your words as you wrote them.

We do not use your questions to train any AI model.

Data sent outside Brazil

Your account, your match backup and contributed games are stored in Brazil. Two more things leave Brazil, each only after you agree to that transfer on its own:

The United States does not have a data-protection law like Brazil's LGPD or the EU's GDPR, and neither Brazil nor the EU has declared that it protects data to the same level. We cannot tell you whether the other countries where Anthropic may process a coach question do, because we do not know in advance which ones they will be. Authorities there may be able to reach the data under US law, and you may find it harder to enforce your rights there. That is why we ask you first.

Under the LGPD, these transfers rest on your specific consent (art. 33, VIII). Under the GDPR, they rest on your explicit consent (art. 49(1)(a)). If you say no, nothing is sent: the coach stays off for you, or the report is not sent. You can withdraw either consent at any time, from then on.

Your rights and controls

In the app (Settings, then Account):

  • Change your choices at any time.
  • Export: one button downloads all of your cloud data as a JSON file. It leaves out the sign-in log described in "How long we keep it"; to receive your entries in it, write to contact@senseitop.com (LGPD art. 18).
  • Delete your cloud history: turns backup off, then removes your backed-up matches. If the delete fails, backup stays off. Turning backup back on uploads every match again.
  • Delete coach history: one answer at a time, or all, once the coach launches.
  • Delete your account: removes your profile and every row you own: matches, coach history, usage rows and your entries in the sign-in log. Reports at Sentry are not deleted one by one; they expire as "How long we keep it" says.

Deleting your account never touches the database on your computer.

Contributed games are the exception. Nothing in a contributed game says who sent it, and neither we nor your computer keep anything that tells which games are yours. So we cannot show you your contributed games, give you a copy, correct them or delete them, and neither the export nor deleting your account reaches them. Where we cannot tell whose data something is, the GDPR does not ask us to collect more information just to answer a request (art. 11), and Brazil's LGPD does not treat data that cannot reasonably be linked to a person as personal data (art. 12). We treat contributed games as your data all the same, and rely on your consent to receive them. Your control comes before sending: your yes also sends the games already in your history, so decide before you give it; and you can turn Help build the stats off at any time, after which nothing more is sent.

Under Brazil's LGPD (art. 18) you have the right to: confirm whether we process your data; access it; correct it; have unnecessary, excessive or unlawfully processed data anonymised, blocked or deleted; take it to another provider; delete data processed with your consent; know who we share it with; know what happens if you refuse consent; and withdraw consent. You may also ask for a review of decisions made solely by automated means (art. 20); the coach's answers are not decisions about you, but the right stands.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the GDPR (or its local version) gives you similar rights: access, correction, deletion, restriction, portability, objection to processing based on legitimate interest, and withdrawal of consent.

To use any of these rights, use the buttons above or write to contact@senseitop.com. We answer within 15 days. We may ask you to confirm the request from your account's email address, so no one else can ask for your data.

You can complain to Brazil's data-protection authority, the ANPD, or, outside Brazil, to the data-protection authority where you live. We would like the chance to fix it first, so please write to us too.

Security

Connections are encrypted. Each player can read only their own rows in our database, and no player can read contributed games. Your sign-in session on your computer is encrypted by your system, and is not kept at all where your system offers no encryption. Error reports are scrubbed before they leave your computer. Your Arena and Magic Online logs never leave your computer. No system is perfectly safe; if a breach puts you at risk, we will tell you and the ANPD as the law requires.

What we never do

These always hold:

  • We never sell your data.
  • We never show ads, and never use your data for advertising.
  • We never upload your Arena or Magic Online logs.
  • We never use your data to train an AI model.
  • We never turn on any of the account's choices, or contributing your games, for you.

Changes to this policy

When this policy changes, we change the date at the bottom of this page, and that date becomes its new version. Each new version asks signed-in players their account's choices again, the next time they use the app signed in, before it applies to them. If a version changes what the account collects or why, the app also says what changed. A yes to contributing your games keeps running when this policy changes: each game is sent labelled with the version you said yes under. If a change adds to what a record holds, the new fields serve only the same stats, and the app does not ask again: we say what changed here first, and you can turn Help build the stats off at any time. If a change alters what the games are used for, the app asks you again before it sends another game, and the change applies only to games sent after you agree. Earlier versions are kept in the project's public history.
