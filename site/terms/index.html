The rules coach

The rules coach comes with the account. Once you agree to these terms, it adds rules notes to the reports you write in the app while you are signed in, until you turn rules notes off. If you do not want it, turn rules notes off in Settings, under Account, or use the app without an account: everything on your computer works the same. Your rights over what the coach handles, and how to use them, are in the Privacy Policy.

  • What it does. When you press Write it with Claude or Copy the prompt on a match report, a deck guide, an RCQ guide or the cross-deck coach report, the app writes up to 4 questions about how the cards in it work, and puts them to the rules coach. The answers are kept on your computer and quoted in the report as rules notes, with the rules they cite.
  • It is an AI and can be wrong. The app shows every rules note with this line: "AI answer, may be wrong. Check the cited rules; at a tournament, ask a judge." Read the cited rules before you rely on a note.
  • It is not a judge. A note is not an official ruling. At a tournament, the judge's ruling is the one that counts. Wizards of the Coast does not write, check or endorse the coach's answers.
  • Limits. To keep the coach free, it has a shared monthly budget and a daily limit for each account. When either runs out, or when we pause the coach to fix a problem, the report is written without rules notes, and the app says so.
  • The limits may change. We may change them.

Sending the coach's questions to Anthropic, in the United States

When you agree to these terms at sign-in, you also agree to this transfer. It is the only consent these terms ask for: cloud backup and error reports are asked on their own, and stay off until you say yes.

What is sent. Each question the app writes: the names of cards from your deck and from opposing decks, and a question about how they work together, in English. The questions carry nothing else about your match: not who played, when, the result, any chat, or your Arena or Magic Online name or your opponent's. Each request also carries your sign-in, so our coach server knows which account is asking; the sign-in is not passed on to Anthropic. Anthropic receives the question and a one-way code made from your account's internal ID, so it can help detect misuse coming from one account; it cannot turn the code back into your ID. Anthropic never receives your name, your email address or your Google or Discord account ID.

Where it goes, and who handles it. First to our coach server, which runs on Google Cloud in São Paulo, Brazil, and finds the rules and card texts that match each question. Then the question and those texts go to Anthropic, PBC, a US company, whose Claude models write the answer.

Anthropic acts as our processor, under its Commercial Terms and its Data Processing Addendum: it uses the question to answer it and to enforce its Usage Policy, and its terms do not allow it to train its models on what we send. Anthropic stores the question and its answer in the United States, and may process them in any country where it runs its models; it does not name those countries in advance. It deletes them within 30 days. If its automated safety systems flag a question as breaking its Usage Policy, it may keep the question and the answer for up to 2 years, and the safety scores it gave them for up to 7 years. It may also keep them longer if the law requires.

Why. Only to write the rules notes in your report. We do not use the questions to train any AI model.

The risk. The United States has no general data-protection law like Brazil's LGPD or the EU's GDPR. Brazil has not declared that it protects data to the same level; the EU has done so only for US companies certified under the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, and we do not rely on that here. We cannot say whether the other countries where Anthropic may process a question offer that protection, because we do not know in advance which ones they will be. Authorities there may be able to reach the data, and you may find it harder to enforce your rights there.

What we keep. Not the text of any question or answer: the answers stay on your computer. For each question the app sends, the coach keeps a record with:

  • your account and the day;
  • whether the question was answered;
  • the model and settings used, how long the question and the answer were, the cost and the time taken;
  • the version of the rules, and how many citations were wrong.

It also keeps a count of your questions for the day. We keep these records in our account database, at Supabase, in São Paulo, to hold the limits and the budget and to check how well the coach answers. They are deleted after 90 days, or when you delete your account, whichever comes first, and your data export includes them. Google keeps the coach server's request logs, with your IP address, in Brazil, for 30 days.

The legal basis. The coach is part of the account you asked for; sending its questions abroad rests on your consent.

  • Under the LGPD, it rests on your specific consent (art. 33, VIII), and it is also needed to provide the rules coach that comes with the account (art. 33, IX).
  • Under the GDPR, it rests on your explicit consent (art. 49(1)(a)). For readers in the EU, Anthropic's Data Processing Addendum also includes the EU's standard contractual clauses (art. 46(2)(c)). They bind Anthropic by contract, but cannot stop US authorities from reaching the data.

You give your consent by ticking the box that agrees to these terms when you sign in.

How to stop. You can stop at any time: turn off Add rules notes to my reports in Settings, under Account, and the app asks the coach nothing from then on. Signing out stops it too, and deleting your account ends it for good. Stopping does not undo what was sent before, and the app keeps writing reports, without rules notes.
